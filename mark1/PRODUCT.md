# Working product direction

Working name: **Service Stories**. Restaurant: Saint Bart’s. We have not chosen the final product name.

Description: **A shared kitchen memory that helps the team know what is ready, what is missing, and what to do next.**

The experience starts with the menu. Answering one small question at a time builds a visual kitchen, with cute ingredient icons, a fast staggered menu reveal, restrained press/hover responses, selected glass surfaces, and a compact bottom bar. Team and details open in panels; there is no separate profile-page maze. The existing colors, type and rounded forms are the design reference.

The functional loop is menu → confirmed ingredients and prep → stock changes → expectations → observed outcome → next suggestion. Ordering and prep are different records. A concern is a human observation with optional AI wording and a small proposed action, followed by ownership and an outcome. AI never silently changes stock or treats an estimate as a fact.

Name selection records contributions without claiming strong identity. Team messages are brief and grounded in saved actions, bundled when several arrive. Notifications are in-app for this version.

What we aim to test: fewer missed orders, fewer prep shortages, less avoidable waste, and less effort spent coordinating. None is a demonstrated saving yet. We should collect evidence before claiming money or waste reductions.

What makes Mark 1 useful: a real restaurant can complete the whole loop on real ingredients; records persist; corrections are explainable; the team wants to return. We improve this codebase and migrate its schema rather than create another disposable app.

University presentation is secondary. Later it can describe the problem, data model, decisions, testing, limitations, and observed outcomes honestly. Product functionality comes first.
